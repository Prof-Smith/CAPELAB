A note to students

I have been thinking a lot about valuations recently. Most of the time I avoid making directional market predictions because those forecasts are occasionally right, but often unreliable. The value investor in me still favors proceeding with caution, preserving a margin of safety, and avoiding the temptation to confuse expensive markets with precisely timed crash predictions.

The CAPE ratio, or cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio, compares market prices with inflation-adjusted earnings averaged over the previous ten years. The useful question is not simply whether CAPE is high. The better question is whether high CAPE has historically done a better job warning about lower long-run future returns than forecasting the timing of market downturns.

Student reflection questions

  1. Does a high CAPE ratio reliably predict an imminent market crash?
  2. Does CAPE appear more useful for estimating long-run future returns than short-run market timing?
  3. If CAPE is near a historical extreme, should an investor sell, reduce risk, continue dollar-cost averaging, or ignore valuation?
  4. How would your answer change if your investment horizon were one year, ten years, or thirty years?
  5. What does CAPE help you understand about expected returns, and what does it fail to tell you about timing?
